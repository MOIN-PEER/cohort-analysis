# Key insights (generated from the data by reporting.py)

1. **Retention curve.** 77.4% of customers are still subscribed after month 1, 62.7% after 3 months, 40.3% after 12 and 29.5% after 24. The curve flattens: those who stay past the first few months are much less likely to leave.
2. **Revenue holds up better than customers.** Revenue at month 12 is 56.6% of month-1 revenue, while subscribers are fewer, because loyal customers buy more add-ons.
3. **'First box free' attracts customers who leave.** Only 56.6% stay past month 1 (vs 85.9% with no offer). After 12 months a free-box customer has contributed £37 vs £123 with no offer.
4. **Channels.** Referral and organic customers retain best and pay back fastest. Influencer customers have the highest acquisition cost (£58) and the lowest 12-month LTV:CAC (1.28x, payback month 9), below the common 3:1 rule of thumb.
5. **Price rise.** In 2025-04, churn among established subscribers jumped to 9.85% from a typical 4.93%: about 293 extra cancellations. It appears as a diagonal in the cohort heatmap because it hit every cohort in the same calendar month.
6. **Onboarding worked.** Month-3 retention rose 5.0 points before/after September 2024; controlling for customer mix the estimate is 5.3 points (odds ratio 1.275, 95% CI 1.181-1.376). It is a before/after study, not an A/B test, so other changes at the same time cannot be ruled out.
7. **Forecasting.** A shifted-beta-geometric model fitted on just 6 months of the Jan-Jun 2023 cohorts forecast the next two years with 7.2% average error, against 59.9% for a constant-churn model, so young cohorts' LTV can be projected early.
